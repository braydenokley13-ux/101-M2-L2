Front Office Case File

Small Markets,
Big Money.

A few big-city teams are pulling away from the league. The money gap is getting ugly. The front office needs a revenue-sharing plan — and they want your read.

Your Role

League Revenue Strategist, Bow Sports Capital. New to the room. Sharp on the numbers.

The Mission

Recommend how much money rich teams should share — without blowing up the league.

The Problem

Big markets earn way more than small markets. If the gap grows, games get lopsided and fans leave.

Plain English

Revenue sharing means the league takes some money from every team and spreads it around so smaller teams can keep up. Competitive balance means most teams have a real shot at winning — which is what keeps fans watching.

Step 1 — Read the room

The Situation Board

Five things every front office watches before a big money call. Read them. They all pull in different directions.

01

What fans care about

Close games. Fans show up when their team has a real shot. Blowouts empty the seats.

02

What ownership wants

Big-market owners say it straight: "We earned this money. Why pay for our rivals?"

03

What small markets risk

Without help, small-city teams can't afford star players. They fall behind — and some even leave town.

04

What the league earns

More teams in the race = more national interest = a bigger TV deal for everyone. A boring league shrinks the money.

05

What sponsors notice

Sponsors pay for eyeballs. Empty arenas and one-sided games make them nervous.

Why this matters

You can't make everyone happy at once. Help one side and you put pressure on another. That tension is the job.
